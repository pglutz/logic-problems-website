## Statement

Suppose that $E$ is a CBER and there are hyperfinite CBERs $E_0 \subseteq E_1 \subseteq E_2 \subseteq \ldots$ such that $E = \bigcup_{n \in \N} E_n$. Must $E$ be hyperfinite?

## Definitions

A *CBER*, hort for *countable Borel equivalence relation* is an equivalence relation $E$ on a Polish space $X$ such that $E$ is a Borel subset of $X^2$ and each equivalence class of $E$ is countable.

A CBER $E$ on $X$ is *hyperfinite* if there are CBERs $E_0 \subseteq E_1 \subseteq E_2 \subseteq \ldots$ such that $E = \bigcup_{n \in \N}E_n$ and for each $n$, all of $E_n$'s equivalence classes are finite.

Sometimes a CBER which is an increasing union of countably many hyperfinite CBERs is called *hyperhyperfinite.* Hence the question can be restated as asking whether every hyperhyperfinite CBER is hyperfinite.

## Known Partial Results

Conley, Jackson, Marks, Seward and Tucker-Drob [^CJMSTD23] showed that if a CBER $E$ is a generated by a graph which can be written as a countable increasing union of graphs of finite Borel asymptotic dimension (a stronger assumption than hyperfiniteness) then $E$ is hyperfinite.
